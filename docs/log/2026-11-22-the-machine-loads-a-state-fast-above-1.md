`LoadOptions.Fast` was the caller's choice, and spin offered it as a host policy, `fast_load`, off by default, whose comment cited 293 against 152 ms from before mapped-ram and multifd (lab 44585840498). A fast load reached the first login at ~400 ms against ~95 for a full one at today's state (lab 44987275543). *The crossover:* `restore-first` gained `touched=MIB`, which writes that many MiB of non-zero bytes into a tmpfs in the guest after its cold login and before its save. Bench, nested, spin's shape and login, plugged to 2048 MiB up to 1.5 GiB touched, then to 4096 and 8192, the state evicted, 20 a side interleaved, first login p50/p95 in full against fast: 179 MiB of data 95/105 against 489/563, +394 [+388, +420]; 825 MiB 258/310 against 431/446, +173 [+149, +195]; 1.2 GiB 374/510 against 476/534, +103 [+88, +120]; 1.7 GiB 570/763 against 441/498, −129 [−167, −76]; 3.7 GiB 1410/1804 against 451/518, −959 [−1008, −911]; 7.3 GiB 3152/3658 against 538/581, −2615 [−2642, −2589]. A full load costs the disk's read of the data, ~0.4 ms a MiB; a fast load's login barely moves. *Where a fast load's time goes* (branch `exp-470-blocktime`, `postcopy-blocktime` on, lab 45035029887): at 179 MiB ~2,500 faults, mean 69 µs, most 64-128 µs, blocked the vCPU 45 ms of the ~400 the login adds. With the state read ahead each fault took 5.5 µs and blocktime 3 ms, and the login was still 325 ms (−168 against no read-ahead, +234 over a full load). The postcopy lasted 0.8 s at 179 MiB and 2.4 s at 3.7 GiB: QEMU's eager thread places every 4 KiB page of the RAM blocks at their ceilings, 8 GiB, one ioctl each. That this is what slows the guest is inferred, not measured (#519). Postcopy's preempt channel is a second socket from a source, for pages queued behind the precopy stream, and a file load has neither. *Read-ahead during a fast load* (lab 45035048486, 10 a side, 3.7 GiB): faults 78 against 85 µs, login 436 against 458, p95 713 against 490, so it is not carried. *Changed:* `LoadOptions.Fast` and `spin-machine restore --fast` are gone. `Resume` loads fast a mapped-ram state with more than `machine.fastAbove` (1536 MiB) of data on a host that grants QEMU a userfaultfd (`/dev/userfaultfd` open to every user, spin's grant, or `vm.unprivileged_userfaultfd=1`), and in full otherwise. The read-ahead and the channels follow the same choice. `restore-first` lost its `fast` row; a bench runner grants the userfaultfd, so `touched` over 1.5 GiB measures the fast load. `boot:record`'s events test fills 1.6 GiB of a 2 GiB guest for the fast load's events. spin's `fast_load` is spin-stack/spin#1570. *Status:* in place.
