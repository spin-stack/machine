A host whose state changed during the run, which only a burst feels. `restore-together` now records what the host counted during each burst (PSI stall time of CPU, I/O and memory; `/proc/vmstat`'s direct reclaim, compaction stalls and huge-page fallbacks, MiB read; CPU busy; memory free and cached going in) and every restore's `running`, and takes `states=cached` (the disk out of the burst) and `defrag` (the host's THP defrag policy for the burst). *Laptop, i9-13900HK, not nested, 5-6 bursts a row, leads only:* 8 at once from the disk read 590-740 ms against 107-110 alone, the disk the bound (1.4 GiB at ~2.7 GB/s, I/O PSI full ~120-260 ms a burst) with or without huge pages. From the page cache: 440-445 ms under the default `madvise`, with ~3000 compaction stalls that all failed, ~2.4 GiB scanned by direct reclaim and memory PSI full ~250 ms a burst, and 85% of 20 CPUs busy; 133 ms with huge pages off for QEMU (PR_SET_THP_DISABLE, a wrapper, Δ −307 [−437, −156]); 150 under `defrag=defer` with no compaction and memory PSI 3 ms. Alone, huge pages off cost +16.0 [+8.9, +22.6] after running (the guest on 4 KiB pages), and `defer` 74.5 → 80.3 (5 each). QEMU marks guest RAM MADV_HUGEPAGE, so under `madvise` every fault of a load that finds no free 2 MiB block compacts and reclaims before it returns: one restore finds the ~90 blocks it needs, eight need ~700 at once and fight over them, and a host that has run a while (a page cache full of states, the bench after its first ~20 GiB) has none to spare. spin restores workspaces concurrently (spin-stack/spin at 1bd3414: an actor goroutine per workspace in `internal/runner/reconcile`, a per-workspace lock in `workspace.Manager.Resume`, up to 10 commands at once in `handler`), and leaves the host's THP policy as the distribution sets it. *On the bench* (lab 44810128678, v20261120.03, spin's shape, 8 channels, 30 bursts a row, every restore one sample): under `defer` against `madvise`, 1 at once 99.6 → 99.5, Δ −0.1 [−2.0, +1.6]; 8 at once from the disk 283/384 → 239/281, Δ −44.1 [−53.2, −35.2]; from the page cache 1 at once 72.3 → 72.0, 8 at once 268/317 → 193/241, Δ −74.9 [−83.2, −64.9]. *Decided:* THP defrag `defer` is a requirement of spin's host (`/sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/defrag`; `docs/memory-and-disk.md`): it gains in a burst and costs one restore alone nothing, spin never sets the policy and Ubuntu's default is `madvise`. *Not explained:* 8 at once under `defer` still read ~190-240 against ~100 alone, and from the cache barely less than from the disk. `restore-together` now records where a burst ran on the host's CPUs: each CPU's busy time (/proc/stat) and interrupts by line (/proc/interrupts), the CPUs QEMU's running threads were on, sampled every 5 ms, and the QEMUs', the job's CPU lists and irqbalance. It records them because nothing pins QEMU (spin-machine runs it with `-sandbox resourcecontrol=deny`, spin sets only a cgroup `cpu.weight`) and one CPU taking the disk's interrupts or the threads would look like this. *Where on the host's CPUs* (lab 44817486118, v20261120.04, 20 bursts): not one CPU.
