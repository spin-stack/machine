The boot chart samples every 5 ms, sees only processes and draws nothing of the login's ~65 ms after sshd's accept. The `ledger` probe records the scheduler's tracepoints from the kernel's start and cuts the kernel's clock into windows where the boot and the login change hands, each with its CPU by unit and its idle. The host's clock meets the kernel's through markers the guest writes on request, to ±0.15 ms. *The premise that broke:* the release kernel forces lockdown at confidentiality, which takes tracefs away (lab 45061089609), so the ledger runs on a kernel built from branch `ledger-533-kernel` with lockdown forced to none (parts run 45061289985), and no release gets a ledger until #536 decides how. *Cost* in spin's shape, 20 a side (lab 45063070048): first login +3.0 [−2.4, +15.9], within noise; console prompt +12.1 [+7.1, +17.6]; the kernel alone −1.0 [−9.5, +4.9]. *What six boots of v20261122.05 show* (labs 45062548343, 45062836688, bench, spin's shape): the kernel to PID 1's exec 83 ms, 72 of it in PID 1 before its exec, which is why the boot chart's init.scope (118-123 ms) is twice the ledger's 59 from PID 1's exec on. From PID 1 to the login's sshd-session, 171 ms: PID 1 55, idle 18, journald 18, unattributed (generators and executors) 17, logind 15. Between 110 and 151 ms, the stretch #521 read as the vCPU nearly idle on the disk, PID 1 runs 26 to 30 ms and the vCPU idles 7 to 13 while tasks wait on the disk. The login's 65 ms from sshd-session's exec to true's exit has no idle in it: sshd 20 ms, user@0.service 17, udevd 9, PID 1 4, journald 3. Root's manager (linger) starts as the connection arrives, and its 17 ms of CPU land inside the login's windows (#527). *Opened:* #536, #537. *Status:* in place.
