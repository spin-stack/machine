A number said how long a boot took, and nothing showed what ran in it. The `bootchart` edit puts systemd-bootchart (Ubuntu's 235-2build1, pinned in versions.yaml) into a tree's overlays as PID 1, sampling at 200 Hz. The `bootchart` probe stops it with the first login and keeps its chart and `systemd-analyze plot` beside `result.json`. `releases measure` draws each release once, and its record links both charts from docs/releases.md. *Cost* (bench, spin's shape, 20 a side, v20261122.03, lab 45036503632): first login +10.4 [+4.5, +16.8], console prompt +15.7 [+12.3, +19.7], QEMU RSS +4.0 MiB. It is a diagnostic and never in the image. *Fixed on the way:* the sampler is named `init`, not systemd-bootchart (lab 45035203425 found none); the stop command's sudo and its forks drew themselves ~0.1 s past the login (labs 45036002014, 45036494125); 235 names processes from `/proc/PID/sched` as "9" on this kernel and reads cgroup v1, so it names them by command line and draws no cgroup. *What v20261122.03's chart shows* (lab 45037354657, 3 boots): PID 1 at 82 ms of the kernel's clock and the login at 320. From about 100 to 140 ms the vCPU is nearly idle while the disk reads ~230 MB/s, so systemd's first 40 ms wait on the image. From about 190 ms to the login the one vCPU is busy and every process waits to run. udevd (21.5 ms), journald (18.6) and logind (14.8) use the most CPU. In the plot, systemd-udevd.service activates for 189 ms and systemd-udev-trigger.service for 127. These are leads, and the log's 2.1 is why a chart's bar is not a saving. *Status:* in place.
