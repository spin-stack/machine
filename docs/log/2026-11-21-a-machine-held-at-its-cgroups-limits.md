spin sets `cpu.max`, `memory.high` or `memory.max`, and `io.max` on a workspace's cgroup, and the limit applied silently. A host CPU Stall could be the host squeezing the machine or spin's own `cpu.max`. *Built:* the host side of the probe reads QEMU's cgroup each second, beside its PSI triggers, and tells the watch a `Limited` each second a limit held: `cpu.max` when `nr_throttled` moved, `memory.high`, `memory.max` and its OOM kill when `memory.events` moved, and a key of `io.max` when a device's rate reached 90% of it. Each carries the limit, its device, the limit's value, what was used, how many times the kernel held the machine, and since when. The last reading is taken at QEMU's exit. `Usage` gains `CPU.Throttles` and `memory.events`' four counters. *Checked first:* cgroup v2 has no counter of what `io.max` throttled. `io.stat` gained no field on Linux 7.0 with `CONFIG_BLK_DEV_THROTTLING=y`, under an `io.max` that held `dd` for 6.4 s. *Observed on real QEMU* (task boot:pressure, laptop, v20261120.04 with this checkout's sender, one vCPU, 1 GiB). Under `cpu.max` of 0.2 CPU, one loop brought a `Limited` each second at 199,500-200,200 µs a second and 10 throttles, and a host CPU Stall each second with it. With `memory.high` 32 MiB above QEMU's memory, rereading a second disk brought 2,200-4,300 `high` events a second, `memory.current` within 0.1% of the limit. Under an `rbps` of 4 MiB/s, QEMU read at 4,188,788-4,189,940 B/s. Its cgroup's `io.pressure` total did not move in those 5 s, because io_uring leaves no QEMU thread waiting, so the rate is the one sign. No phase before the limits brought a `Limited`. *Not measured:* boot and restore. The limits are read in a watch's own goroutine, and neither goal measurement starts a watch. *Status:* in place.
