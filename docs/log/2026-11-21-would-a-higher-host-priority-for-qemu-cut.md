A weight, a nice value or a real-time class changes only who runs when two threads want one CPU. So the most any of them can win is the time QEMU's threads spend runnable and not running, and that was measured before anything was built. `run-delay` samples every QEMU thread's `/proc/PID/task/TID/schedstat` every 5 ms, from before the exec to the first login, cold and restored (evicted). It keeps an exited thread's last sample and splits the wait at the guest's first run, from QEMU's trace. Its rows are crowds of workspaces already running, as `density` boots them. *The instrument sees what it claims:* the guest's steal time, which KVM takes from the vCPU thread's run_delay, read 330-340 ms cold on the busy host against the vCPU thread's 304-331. *Idle host* (bench, spin's shape and login, v20261121.01, 20 boots, 0 lost; lab 44925573288): QEMU's threads wait 0.2/1.1 ms in all to the cold first login (467/479) and 0.1/0.2 to the restored one (115). That is the ceiling of any priority change on the host the goal is measured on, and it is under the noise by ~40×. *Busy host,* 16 workspaces each keeping its vCPU busy on the bench's 16 CPUs: the cold first login reads 1184/1490 and the restored 306/321. QEMU's threads wait 824 ms cold, summed over threads that wait at the same time (331 of it the vCPU's, 248 the main thread's, the rest vhost and io_uring workers), and 282 restored, 224 of it before the guest runs. The host's own steal read 0 in every row, so the bench's hypervisor (L0) took nothing visible at 10 ms ticks. Only the host's (L1's) wait is in reach of a priority, and that is what was measured. *The arm* (lab 44927312303, 20 boots a row, interleaved): the crowd at nice 10 against the crowd at 0. The lab runs unprivileged, and 10 steps either way is the same ratio of weights (110:1024 against 1024:9548), so this is QEMU at −10 as the scheduler sees it. The restored first login fell −79.3 [−93.5, −71.0] (316 → 238) and its wait 290 → 116 ms. The cold first login moved −104.7 [−258.3, +150.9], within noise: the vCPU's wait fell 304 → 119 ms, but the main thread's rose 208 → 289 and the vhost workers' 90 → 122. QEMU's own CPU time grows from 503 ms idle to ~605 busy, which no priority wins back. *Decided:* nothing changes in the machine. On the idle host there is nothing to win. On a busy host, restores gain clearly, and the lever, a workspace's cgroup `cpu.weight`, is spin's runner's (machine.Start's `Cmd` places QEMU in it). That is a proposal for spin ("Open experiments"), to be measured there. The lab put every QEMU in one cgroup, where each thread competes on its own. In spin each workspace is a cgroup whose weight its threads share, which may give less. SCHED_FIFO was not tried, because a weight already shows the gain and a FIFO QEMU could starve the host. *Status:* the probe is kept, so the wait can be read again on a host whose load is spin's.
