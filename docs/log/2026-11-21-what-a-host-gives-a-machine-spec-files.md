spin hard-coded two facts that depend on the machine and its release. It linked a release's whole firmware directory into each QEMU's root, and it bounded each QEMU's cgroup at the guest's ceiling plus 768 MiB and at its vCPU ceiling plus 256 tasks. *Traced* (strace of a KVM and a TCG boot): QEMU opens by name its binary, the kernel, the initrd, and from the firmware directory the BIOS, `linuxboot_dma.bin` and `kvmvapic.bin`. It also opens `/dev/null` for its OFD lock probe. It reads `/proc`, `/sys`, `/dev/urandom` and `qemu.conf` only when they are there. `efi-virtio.rom` is never opened, since every NIC has `romfile=`. *Built:* `Spec.Files`, `Spec.Overhead() (Overhead{Memory, Tasks}, error)`, which reads the kernel's and the initrd's sizes, and `Disk.Overhead`, each qcow2 image's L2 cache (its whole L2 table up to 32 MiB) and four clusters of refcounts, from `read_cache_sizes`. Tasks are counted up to their most: one thread a vCPU slot, QEMU's four, a vhost worker for each device, a checkpoint's channels and their driver, QEMU's thread pool (64, and a thread it cannot start aborts QEMU) and io_uring's workers (the ring's 128 entries). Memory is 64 MiB of QEMU's own, the kernel and the initrd, 1 MiB a vCPU, 64 KiB a thread, and 1/256 of the ceiling for page tables. *Verified* (task boot:overhead, laptop, real KVM, v20261121.01): spin's shape with a NIC and a vsock started as nobody in a root holding only `Files` and `/dev/null`, in a cgroup of its own, and was taken to its ceiling. That is 16 vCPUs, 8192 MiB filled from `/dev/urandom`, and four disks attached beside its two, one of them a 64 GiB overlay the guest wrote a block of every 512 MiB, while the guest read, wrote and trimmed every disk. It was then checkpointed. Beyond the guest it held 31 MiB and 9 tasks at boot, 73 MiB and 27 tasks at the ceiling, and 79 MiB and 34 tasks (pids.peak) through the checkpoint. Of the 73 MiB, 27 were page tables, 16 the bzImage and 8 the big disk's L2 cache. `Overhead` for it, disks included, is 153 MiB and 223 tasks. *Found on the way:* the cgroup's `anon` is not QEMU's memory beyond the guest. A huge page the guest gave back in part stays charged until the kernel splits it, and it read 160 MiB above `smaps` just after the guest freed 1.4 GiB. The lane counts QEMU's own from `smaps`. In 2 of 5 runs, vCPUs plugged right after a checkpoint's `Continue` never reached the guest (#433). The lane plugs before it checkpoints. The checkpoint at the ceiling also got this laptop's QEMU OOM-killed once, by the host, with its swap full. *Not measured:* a restore and a QEMU that has run for days. spin adopts the root and the bounds through spin-stack/spin issues. *Status:* in place.
