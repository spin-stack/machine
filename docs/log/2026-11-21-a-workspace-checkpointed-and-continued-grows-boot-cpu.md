The overhead lane had seen vCPUs plugged right after a checkpoint's `Continue` never reach the guest in 2 of 5 runs, and plugged before its checkpoint since. *Traced* (a miss of the lane in #433's order, its states under `/var/tmp` on ext4, the laptop's disk busy and its swap full, QEMU `-trace` on `cpuhp_acpi_*`, `acpi_gpe_*` and `monitor_qmp_cmd_in_band`): QEMU received no command for 73 s after `cont`. The guest gave up after its 60 s, with `gpe02` at 0 events and no status bit and `sci` at 0, so QEMU had raised nothing. The `device_add`s came after, and the guest took all 15 insert events within 0.4 s. What held the plug was the lane's own helper, which closed the state between `Continue` and the plug. ext4 writes back a file QEMU truncated on its first close, and that close took 0.6 ms to 75 s across 9 checkpoints in the same lane. Nothing was lost in QEMU's hotplug or in the guest's GPE handling. *Ruled out* (laptop, real KVM, v20261121.01 with this checkout's spin-machine, states on tmpfs, so every close is free): 0 misses in 200 guests each way after a checkpoint, after a plain stop and cont, and with no pause. Also 0 in 100 each way after a checkpoint and a stop on two CPUs a spin loop keeps busy, 30 checkpoints with 1400 MiB of the guest filled there, 10 each way at 30% and at 5% of one CPU, 100 checkpoints with #417's close in the save's path again, and 100 each way in a guest whose PID 1 is a shell. The lane in #433's order missed 0 of 50. *Built:* `TestAWorkspaceCheckpointedAndContinuedGrows` (`task boot:cpu-plug`). spin's shape is booted to systemd at 2048 MiB and checkpointed and continued, then given 16 vCPUs, 8192 MiB and four disks at once. The guest must have all of them within 20 s, in 10 guests, and the state is closed once it has. compat.yml runs it, since a QEMU or kernel that lost a hotplug after a migration would leave a workspace at its size, and lab.yml's `check:cpu-plug` runs it on the bench. *Measured:* 10 guests grew in 0.31-0.71 s with the state on tmpfs. With it on ext4 written back at 10 MB/s while the guest grew (a scope's `IOWriteBandwidthMax`), 5 grew in 0.31-1.18 s. `Checkpoint`'s comment now says that the close or sync after `Continue` can take as long as the disk needs, and that a caller must not hold the next command behind it. The lane's comment that blamed the checkpoint is gone. *Status:* in place.
